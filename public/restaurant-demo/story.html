Est. 2019 · Marlow Street

It began with a hole in the roof.

How a leaking print shop became a restaurant with one oven, one menu, and forty seats.

Why We’re Here

We wanted to run the kind of place we’d want to eat at on our night off.

Small enough to know everyone who walks in. Short enough a menu that the kitchen can cook it properly. And a fire — because food cooked over oak tastes like someone cared.

i.

The Idea

Daniel Reyes and Mara Ellison met in other people’s kitchens — he on the line at hotels along Roxas Boulevard, she running the floor in Makati — and kept coming back to the same conversation: restaurants had gotten big, loud, and long. Ten-page menus, freezers full of maybe.

In early 2019 they found an old print shop on Marlow Street in Pasig with a leaking roof and rent to match. The landlord agreed to knock a little off if they stopped the leak themselves. They stopped it by putting a hole in it — for the flue of a hand-built oven weighing well over a tonne.

Fourteen months later, the doors opened with a menu of nine dishes. Six years on, it has never been longer than twelve.

ii.

The Ingredients

We buy from growers within a few hours’ drive — Benguet vegetables, Nasugbu beef, Mindoro fish — most of it at the Saturday market in Pasig. Whatever comes back that day becomes next week’s menu — which is why the tanigue might vanish by Friday and why we can’t promise the mango dessert will still exist by June.

It means buying less, more often, and paying our farmers fairly for it. It also means the people who grow our food sometimes end up eating at table twelve, which is exactly how it should be.

“If the growers have a good week, the menu has a good week.”
Mara Ellison · Co-founder

iii.

The Fire

There is no gas in the kitchen. Oak only — raked into embers rather than burned as flames, because embers forgive and flames don’t. Nearly everything on the plate touches smoke at some point, even if it’s just the butter.

The oven goes down overnight but never fully cools. It wakes up with whoever opens at ten, the way it has since March 2019 — holidays excepted, and even then it’s debatable.

iv.

The Room Tonight

Forty seats, ten of them at the counter facing the fire. The servers know every dish because most of them help plate it; there’s no pass theatre, just a kitchen small enough to talk over.

The print-shop past hasn’t been scrubbed out either — the type drawers behind the bar still hold the corks, and the floor still slopes slightly toward the old press room. We think that’s part of the flavour.

House Rules

Four things we believe.

  1. Cook over fire, season with patience.

    Most of our cooking takes longer than the guest does. That’s fine. Twelve hours for a short rib, four minutes for a tanigue — each gets exactly what it needs.

  2. Buy less, more often.

    A short menu changes weekly because the market does. If we run out, we’d rather tell you than reach into a freezer.

  3. Service without theatre.

    Warm, informed, unhurried. Nobody here will describe a carrot to you for ninety seconds.

  4. The bar sits everyone eventually.

    Walk-ins are part of how this room works. Come at seven without a booking and we’ll pour you a glass while we find you somewhere to eat.

Chef & Co-founder

Daniel Reyes

Daniel trained in Basque country kitchens and at hotels in Manila, where he learned that the best tool in any restaurant is a bed of steady embers and the patience to wait for one. He came home to Pasig to build a room around that idea — literally, helping to lay the oven brick by brick.

He still cooks six services a week and writes the menu each Thursday with whatever Mara carried back from the market. His favourite seat in the house is counter stool three, from which he pretends not to watch the pass.

— Daniel
